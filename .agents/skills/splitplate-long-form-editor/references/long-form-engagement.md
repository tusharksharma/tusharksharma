## Apply to future Split Plate edits

### Opening and story

Start with a strong real food action or result and one specific viewer promise. Establish the dinner's practical question early: what deserves the effort, how the shortcut works, or how different plates share one cook. Use the actual footage to choose the opening; no invented conflict, exaggerated result or long biography.

As an editorial starting point, aim to communicate the promise within roughly 15 seconds and begin useful process shortly afterward. This is our proposed target, not a measured Sam timing rule. A brief teaser may reveal the result while saving the explanation and honest verdict for later.

Write one main question with a visible final answer. Give each cooking section its own small payoff: a crust reveal, consistency check, useful shortcut, plate split, or genuine reaction. Keep pending questions in the beat plan and resolve them. Do not keep delaying useful information just to create suspense.

### Cuts and coverage

Cut because the information, action, viewpoint or emotion changes. Show one representative repeat clearly, then compress the rest with truthful elapsed-time cues. Preserve enough demonstration for the viewer to understand the method. Avoid filling the requested runtime with repetitive stirring or narration.

Alternate existing POV food/hands, useful close-ups, and genuine recorded speech when available. Use a crop change on the same camera only when it reveals detail and remains sharp; do not fake a second camera or use incessant zooms to imitate coverage. Preserve original 4K and approximately 60 fps delivery rules.

During an edit review, flag long stretches with no new action, information or feeling. A useful starting check is every 20–40 seconds, not a requirement to add an effect on a timer. Let important cooking actions and genuine conversation last as long as they earn.

For future shoots, capture a clean finished-food action, key technique close-ups, a brief face-to-camera thought, actual tasting, and both plates when relevant. Treat missing coverage as a pickup suggestion; never manufacture a taste verdict or family response.

### Original graphics and animation options

Use the established Split Plate type and assets. Prefer a brief dish title over real food, compact ingredient/checkpoint labels, a simple elapsed-time cue, and a purposeful adult/kid plate comparison. Keep text away from hands and the food being explained.

For the next edit, test a restrained slide/fade for a chapter label, a small pop-in on one key takeaway, or a line/arrow that identifies the exact texture being discussed. Starting proposals: approximately 0.2–0.35-second entrances and 2–3-second text holds, extended when reading needs it. These are our original design settings, not verified Sam measurements. Avoid constant bouncing captions, a full-screen scrim, or a lengthy animated intro.

A brief monochrome teaser is an optional deliberate treatment, not a new default grade. Keep the normal film naturally colored. Do not copy Sam's chef figure, logo, subscription animation, branded assets, jokes, scripts or exact sequence.

### Sound and personal presence

Prioritize genuine synchronized pan sizzle, chopping, pouring and plating. Leave intentional short narration gaps when real sound supplies the information. Duck any licensed or user-owned music under speech and important kitchen sounds; assess the actual mix rather than applying one gain to everything.

A subtle original whoosh or chime may punctuate a meaningful graphic or chapter change. It is an option to audition, not a confirmed observation about these references. Do not layer effects onto every cut or add artificial crunch/sizzle as evidence of a food result. Never extract another creator's soundtrack for reuse.

Keep Marcus speaking as Tushar. Pair concise practical explanation with confirmed personal choices and usable recorded speech. Preserve real spontaneous humor when relevant; do not imitate Sam's persona, transplant his family experiences, or invent banter. Keep the shared-family meal and convenience philosophy central.

### Payoff, review and learning

Show the finished food clearly, the actual plate split where relevant, and an honest supported verdict. Close the opening question before one relevant CTA. The last minute should deliver something beyond a recap.

Save a beat plan with source in/out, viewer question, new information/payoff, narration, real sound, proposed graphic and reason for each major transition. Review the encoded opening, technique reveals, sound gaps, crop changes and final payoff as moving picture and audio.

For the next film, use the food-first promise and recurring practical reveals as the main experiment. Log additional graphics and sounds as creative choices, not evidence of a retention improvement. Compare YouTube's first-30-second retention, average percentage viewed, and actual dips/spikes against similar long-form films at the same age when analytics are available. Keep missing data blank; change one main variable at a time and retain what our audience supports.
