# Evidence-based video analysis and production planning

Use for a reference breakdown, review of an existing edit, or pre-production informed by a reference. A reference is optional; do not add competitor research to every recipe request.

## Obtain and describe the evidence

Use the supplied file, authorized media connection, or accessible source URL. Record source identity, version, duration, and what was actually accessible: video playback, sampled frames, audio, timed transcript, or text only. If access fails, produce only the supported partial analysis and request the missing asset. Do not bypass access controls or claim to have watched a video from its caption.

Use available media tools rather than assuming Ootto, Obsidian, Whisper, yt-dlp, or an MCP server is installed. Do not install or execute an upstream installer merely to follow this reference. For public third-party videos, retain source links and brief evidence excerpts; do not republish full transcripts or copy footage into the final recipe edit without appropriate rights.

For frame-based review, record each image's source time and inspect it as an image. Sample the opening more closely, then representative process, split, and payoff moments. Declare gaps. Inspect adjacent frames or actual playback before making claims about motion or precise cut timing. A contact sheet is an overview, not proof of full playback review.

Preserve transcript cue start/end times, source language, and whether captions are human-authored or automatic. Check recipe quantities and ingredient names against the recipe. Align speech and visuals using their actual time coordinates; document offsets instead of estimating them from text length. A silent video can receive a visual analysis with audio marked absent. An untimed transcript cannot support exact speech-to-frame claims.

## Analysis output

Use the analysis template in [record-templates.md](record-templates.md). Separate three kinds of statements:

- Observation: something seen, heard, or present in supplied metrics, with a time or source reference.
- Interpretation: a possible explanation, explicitly marked as a hypothesis.
- Recommendation: a change supported by the recipe, available footage, and house rules.

Describe the opening visual, overlay, spoken line, and immediate viewer benefit separately. Break the sequence into meaningful beats with time ranges, action, text, audio, and payoff. Review food visibility, text readability, the shared cook and split where relevant, dead time, audio balance, and whether the ending fulfills the opening promise.

Conclude with a few transferable techniques and one proposed original application. Borrow techniques such as showing a texture change before explaining it; do not reproduce another creator's script, identity, claimed experiences, or exact visual sequence. A high view count does not establish why a reference succeeded.

## Hook selection and storyboard

When alternatives are useful, prepare two or three different mechanisms, such as sensory result, practical constraint, or a real comparison. For each, specify the source shot/time range, overlay, proposed speech, claim evidence, viewer benefit, and intended payoff. Do not invent fear, family conflict, nutrition, or a numerical promise to fit a hook pattern. Rank for evidence, clarity, brand fit, and available footage; any score is editorial judgment, not a predicted reach number.

Connect each beat to a useful visual and recipe step. A shot can earn its place through sensory sound, process proof, or a finished-food reveal even without narration. Do not turn an instruction into a text card if a real demonstration is available.

Maintain both narrative order and efficient filming order. Group pickups by cooking stage and setup, identify essential coverage, and link each shot back to its beat. Avoid suggesting stock footage of another dish as evidence that Tushar cooked this one. Record honest missing coverage instead.

Plan companion cuts against the existing source-allocation map. This guide does not permit overlapping source ranges where the house rules prohibit them. Platform adaptations should address actual framing, readable text, metadata, CTA destinations, and available audio rights; do not impose unverified rules about what every platform's viewers want.

## Review an existing render

Tie each issue to a time range, evidence, impact, and smallest correction. Compare narration cues with the action actually visible. Keep creative scores separate from measured retention. Do not infer a drop-off point without a retention curve. Return the review only unless revisions were requested.
