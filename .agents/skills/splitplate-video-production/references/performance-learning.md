# Learning from recipe videos and audience feedback

Use when real analytics, feedback, or published examples are supplied or accessible within the task. Read `docs/video/REELRISE_LEARNING_MODEL.md` and the current content strategy before recommendations. Preserve active experiments; do not import Ootto's posting frequency or replace the user's calendar.

## Build a comparable cohort

Record the export or API source, retrieval time, coverage period, pagination completeness, and metric definitions. Use a dated export honestly when live access is unavailable; do not describe it as current. Count distinct post IDs, exclude duplicates, and keep an exclusion log. Separate organic from promoted results and different platforms, formats, series, purposes, and post-age windows. Do not treat missing data as zero.

Use the primary metric appropriate to the post's job and current experiment. Where available, calculate follows, saves, or shares per 1,000 views from the supplied numerator and denominator. Preserve unavailable results for missing or zero denominators. Do not substitute reach for views without renaming the measure. Distinguish account-level follower growth from post-attributed follows.

Prefer aligned-age snapshots, such as each video's first seven days. If only lifetime counts are available, state the age differences and treat comparisons as exploratory. Record sample size, median, range, and notable outliers; avoid selecting only the best-performing posts.

## Classify actual openings

Use the viewed opening and spoken/overlay text when available. A title or caption is only a proxy and must be labeled as such. Suggested categories are sensory result, family-system solution, technique demonstration, experiment, comparison, and personal observation. Allow mixed or uncertain categories rather than forcing every post into a fixed binary split.

Define categories before comparing outcomes, retain underperforming examples, and report exceptions. For each group, show its size, comparable primary-metric median, spread, and evidence IDs. Tiny groups, different recipe appeal, promotion, posting conditions, or incomplete metrics limit conclusions. Even complete separation in a small sample does not prove a general rule or causal effect.

When retention data exists, align actual drop-offs with the video timeline and propose plausible explanations. A drop at a particular second is not automatically a hook, middle, or CTA failure. Without retention data, report visible pacing concerns as editorial observations only.

Return the strongest supported pattern, counterexamples, two practical actions, and what the data cannot establish. Do not invent a minimum view count or declare statistical significance without a suitable analysis.

## Design a useful test

Name the hypothesis, two distinct opening mechanisms, primary metric, comparison window, and decision rule before producing variants. Hold the recipe, approximate length, and other presentation choices reasonably comparable. Organic posts on different dates are a directional experiment, not a randomized A/B test.

Do not publish duplicate cuts or reuse prohibited footage to create a cleaner experiment. Use internal preview alternatives, or fresh/disjoint footage across comparable future recipes, and record the resulting confounds. Reuse of the same body for published variants requires explicit user authorization overriding the applicable footage rule. Never change approved narrative, schedule, or publishing status simply to run a test.

## Audience questions

From supplied or authorized comments, group actual recurring questions, objections, and misunderstandings. Retain evidence IDs and counts with a clear counting unit; distinguish total comments from unique commenters and flag obvious duplicates. Turn supported needs into recipe explanations, pickups, caption clarifications, or content proposals. Frequency in a small sample is not a measurement of the whole audience.

Analyze private messages only when included in the authorized source scope. Keep handles, private message text, personal details, and raw analytics out of the public repository. Use anonymized paraphrases in shared learning notes. This workflow does not send replies or DMs, install comment automation, promise recipe delivery, or publish posts.

## Durable learning without a second analytics system

Use the existing performance sheet and inventory as the measurement records. Store an analysis and learning note with the recipe production or in the user's designated private knowledge folder. Use [record-templates.md](record-templates.md); search notes by series, recipe, hook mechanism, and question before the next related task. Read only relevant notes. If there is no history, say so and use current house rules.

Distinguish `hypothesis`, `observed tendency`, `user-approved rule`, and `retired`. Record evidence, exceptions, scope, date, and next test. An editorial score or external trend report alone cannot promote a hypothesis to an established result. Repeated evidence supports a recommendation; a permanent change to brand or approval rules requires the user's adoption.

Preserve prior conclusions and note what supersedes them. Do not rewrite an old lesson to pretend a new result was known earlier. No analytics have been imported or success patterns established by installing this guide.
