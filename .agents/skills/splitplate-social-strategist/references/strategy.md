# Strategy and editorial decisions

## Goals and positioning

Build recurring audience interest in a useful household food system and the person behind it. Use watch time and follower/subscriber growth as the primary outcome pair. Discuss the tradeoff: a long video can earn more minutes but lose viewers if padded; a viral tip can reach strangers without giving them a reason to follow. Make the promise and recurring value explicit.

Keep the canonical six pillars connected. Fruit preparation can introduce practical parent-focused food knowledge; pancakes demonstrate an enjoyable protein breakfast; a product verdict demonstrates Tushar's buying judgment. Do not narrow the account to the most recent winning product category or force every broad tip to advertise a recipe.

Before recommending a new series, ask what repeatable viewer question it answers, whether the creator has actual evidence/footage, how it differs from existing series, and whether it earns its production time. Improve established series before multiplying labels.

## Capacity and cadence

First inspect the active calendar and ready backlog. Preserve approved releases, series relationships and platform-specific promotion arrangements. The canonical 28 weekly slots are a ceiling, not a quota. Do not automatically add a new schedule on top.

When capacity is unknown, propose a two-week workload trial using the existing backlog. A sustainable fallback to discuss is 5-7 distinct short-form stories weekly, adapted selectively to TikTok and Instagram; 4-6 self-contained Shorts; one useful carousel; Stories around real activity. Long-form can start with one strong film weekly if production permits. Existing approved twice-weekly long-form releases remain in place unless Tushar requests a change. These are provisional editorial options, not platform prescriptions or live scheduling instructions.

Track production minutes, unused footage availability, backlog and completion rate. If capacity is strained, reduce weak flexible slots before weakening the main story or skipping QA. Increase output only when additional posts retain quality and improve the goal metrics per production hour. Select posting times from native audience activity and tests; America/Los_Angeles is the planning timezone until another is requested. Never call a time universally optimal.

## A useful strategy recommendation

Lead with the three highest-priority moves, tied to available evidence. For each include audience problem, example concept, platform/format, opening and payoff, effort, goal metric and uncertainty. Show how one real cooking activity can supply distinct stories with reserved fresh footage. Reuse production effort without duplicating the same visual edit repeatedly on the same platform.

Build reach-to-follow continuity: a broad useful post should make it easy to understand the profile promise and find a relevant next installment. Pin an honest creator introduction, a demonstration of the method and a clear starting resource if current pins need improvement. Do not invent personal origin details or silently change the profile.

## 90-day process

- Days 1-14: establish comparable baselines, check profile clarity and ready inventory, measure work hours, choose one existing experiment to continue or one new hypothesis if none is active.
- Days 15-30: repeat promising concepts with genuinely distinct stories; assess retention and follower conversion at consistent ages. Use measured results rather than declaring a winner after one outlier.
- Days 31-60: expand formats that repeatedly serve the intended audience; test packaging or story depth in sequence. Review the route from Shorts to the corresponding long film separately.
- Days 61-90: evaluate net audience growth, watch time, returning viewers where available and production cost; propose a smaller, stronger recurring program. Update the canonical plan only when requested.

Set numeric targets from the actual baseline and capacity. Do not invent a follower count or promise a percentage improvement before establishing the denominator.
