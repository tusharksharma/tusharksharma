# SplitPlate Production And Scheduling Rules

For landscape long-form YouTube videos, also read `SPLITPLATE_YOUTUBE_VIDEO_RULES.md`. YouTube Shorts continue to use `SPLITPLATE_TIKTOK_EDIT_RULES.md`.

For every short-form video package, read `SPLITPLATE_THUMBNAIL_PLAYBOOK.md` before creating covers. It is authoritative for palette, face/no-face decisions, truthful ImageGen use, clean and creator-style options, and cover QA.

## Audience Target And Organic Discovery

- Core audience: English-speaking parents ages 25-45, led by the United States and including Australia, the United Kingdom, Canada, and comparable developed English-speaking markets.
- Organic posts cannot select an age or country audience the way Promote can. Signal the intended audience consistently through the real situation, opening language, visuals, caption, question, and repeated viewer response; do not use a VPN, fake location, or unsupported regional framing.
- Every package brief must name one primary audience signal before the narrative is approved. Valid signals include family dinner, school lunch, bedtime, workday time pressure, grocery cost, picky preferences, feeding different appetites, fitting training around children, a home-gym system, or a real parent convenience decision.
- Dinner, Quick Lunch, Snack Box, Community, What I Eat, workout, lunchbox, and household-system posts should make the parent or family-life relevance understandable within the first 3 seconds when the footage and story support it. Creami, sauce, product, and technique posts may stay food-first, but should include a truthful parent use case in the opening, narration, caption, or engagement question when one genuinely exists.
- Do not force `busy parents` into every title, fabricate a family problem, show children unnecessarily, or turn an ordinary recipe into parenting advice. The audience signal must come from Tushar's real use and remain secondary when the food itself is the stronger hook.
- Use clear international English and globally recognizable situations. Lead with generic ingredient or meal language unless a brand is the actual subject; avoid country-specific slang, store availability assumptions, and brand-only hooks that make the idea meaningless outside the United States.
- Paid Promote results validate performance with a selected audience but do not prove the same organic distribution. Record promoted and organic results separately and use country, age, retention, saves, shares, comments, and qualified profile visits to evaluate audience fit.

## Package Construction

- Long-form YouTube rule: build in `1920x1080` and default to portrait footage at full height beside useful recipe notes. Expand the canonical recipe into method explanation, likely failure points and fixes, supporting clips, and an honest verdict. Long-form YouTube may reuse source footage from short-form posts, but the finished video must be a materially distinct landscape teaching edit; do not apply the short-form zero-source-overlap rule to it.

- Narrative approval gate: before generating Marcus audio, rendering any final edit, or updating a video to `Ready`, present Tushar with the proposed hook, narrative arc, exact Marcus script, tone, and closing question. Do not treat recipe details, an outline, or a prior package approval as narrative approval. Revise from Tushar's response and obtain explicit approval before rendering.
- Marcus CTA rule: every Marcus video must include a clear, natural call to action. For shorter edits, choose one action that best fits the post - comment, like, save, follow, or visit `thesplitplate.com` - and integrate it into the spoken ending plus the final on-screen text. Every recipe video longer than 40 seconds must include the conversational mid-roll line `By the way, if you've stayed this long and you're enjoying this, give it a like, please. It helps the channel a lot.` near the 30-second mark, then end with one content-specific CTA such as saving the recipe. Keep the two asks separated, never rush them, and do not stack unrelated actions in the closing line.
- Recipe save-sticker rule: end every recipe and recipe-hack video with a three-second animated GIF-style bookmark/save icon and the text `SAVE THIS RECIPE` and `FOLLOW FOR MORE`. Keep the sticker visually prominent over active food footage, pair it with the spoken save/follow close, and do not add a redundant written question when Marcus captions already carry the question.
- Use a raw source range only once across all published, planned, or companion edits. Log five distinct reserve clips for each substantial recipe package before closing it.
- Dinner four-video completion gate: every dinner with enough footage requires exactly four distinct publishable edits before the package is complete: (1) full Split Plate version, (2) full adult version, (3) 15-second discovery/comment-recipe version, and (4) 7-second teaser. Also build two distinct covers and five reserves. Inventory all four videos separately and verify zero raw source-time overlap. Hold the adult cut about 14 days after the Split Plate main and the 15-second cut 7-14 days after the main.
- Complex-recipe comment-cut rule: every recipe with more than five ingredients (six or more, counting the complete served recipe but excluding water and optional garnish) also requires a distinct under-15-second discovery/comment-recipe version. Open on the finished result and confirmed macros, show only the minimum readable method beats, and end with `Comment "recipe" and I'll send it.` Use source ranges that do not overlap the full edit or other companion cuts, inventory it as a separate publishable asset, give it its own discovery caption, and schedule it 7-14 days after the main unless Tushar explicitly chooses another slot. This applies across recipe series, not only dinners.
- Teaser visual rule (locked for future work): every teaser uses B-roll of the final main plated dish and lists the dish's calories and protein on screen. Do not show ingredient prep, cooking steps, packages, alternate plates, child plates, or montage footage. Use source time reserved exclusively for the teaser; never borrow from the main, adult, Split Plate, delayed-comment, carousel, product-review, or reserve timelines. Do not revise an already completed teaser solely to apply this rule retroactively unless Tushar explicitly requests that specific revision.
- Cover completion gate: deliver both clean/editorial and heightened creator-style `1080x1920` JPG options for each publishable video, following `SPLITPLATE_THUMBNAIL_PLAYBOOK.md`. A portrait is earned by the story and a real usable reference; food/action-first covers are equally valid. Keep both options and record their paths in inventory.
- Adult-only framing rule: when a dinner companion shows only the adult meal, do not say or display "adult plate" in audience-facing narration, overlays, hooks, or captions. The absence of the kid plate already establishes the context; keep "adult version" only in internal filenames and inventory labels when needed.
- Macro hook rule: when calories and protein are confirmed for a recipe video, pair them with the opening finished-food visual and state them at the beginning of the narration. Do not hold the macro value until the end. For Split Plate videos, make clear in the broader narrative which serving the macros describe if the distinction could be ambiguous.
- Sensory-proof packaging rule: when the footage supports it, open with real food motion or sound that proves the payoff: a pour, crack, crunch, sizzle, scoop, drizzle, cheese pull, or moving finished plate. Pair that proof with one short macro, time, texture, or practical-use promise that works without sound. Voiceover should add context or curiosity instead of reading the overlay verbatim.
- Micro-payoff pacing rule: remove dead air and repeated mechanics, then give the viewer a meaningful visual change or small payoff roughly every 6-10 seconds in longer edits. Use decisive 1.5-2.5 second action beats when readable, but let finished-food, family, explanation, and sensory moments breathe. Do not force every shot into rapid-fire pacing or add trending music when original kitchen sound is stronger.
- Authentic open-loop rule: dinner-rescue and family-life stories must begin with a real constraint from Tushar's life and resolve it in the same video. Do not invent a crisis, exaggerate conflict with children or schools, moralize food, or withhold the finished dish, verdict, or macros solely to force comments or a part two.
- Schedule a teaser 1-3 days before its main. Do not post a companion carousel within seven days of the main, and do not put a product review built from a dish's footage within seven days of that dish's main.
- Keep main, teaser, and comment-recipe captions purpose-specific. Do not reuse the main caption for companion cuts.
- Delivery rule: whenever a package is completed, include directly watchable links for every publishable video in the chat response, followed by the full-package archive link. Do not deliver only the ZIP or only the main video.
- Agenda synchronization rule: when a video is created for an item already listed on a planning or buffer agenda, update that exact agenda row in the same workflow. Replace stale needs/held status and production notes with the actual completed deliverable details, and keep the agenda status consistent with VIDEO_INVENTORY. A package is not complete until both records are synchronized.
- Updated-recipe return rule: whenever a recipe or package is refreshed, exact-match its slug, title, premise, and prior versions against live TikTok history and POSTING INVENTORY. A correction, cleaner edit, new cover, or small ingredient substitution does not justify reposting the full recipe; update the canonical website/package and keep the existing post as history. A materially changed protein, method, macro profile, serving format, family split, or use case qualifies as a new edition. Preserve the original row as `Posted`, add the new export as a separate `Updated version` inventory row linked to the original post URL/date, summarize what changed, and make the current website recipe canonical. The returning post must explicitly frame the change, use fresh footage or fully disjoint source ranges, and have a distinct hook, cover, narrative, caption, and payoff. Schedule it no earlier than 30 days after the original and behind older ready-unposted mains within the same flexible category unless Tushar explicitly prioritizes it.
- Update VIDEO_INVENTORY, RECIPE VIDEO COVERAGE, UNUSED_CLIP_INVENTORY, and the relevant weekly plan when a package is completed.
- Spoken-brand rule: normal recipe narration and on-screen recipe text use generic ingredient names. Speak a brand only when the post is monetization/product content or the recipe genuinely centers on one specific brand. Keep exact brands in the written recipe package and ingredient source of truth when they document what Tushar used.
- CrumblCreamiCut scores belong only in the weekly ranking video. Save each approved score in private ranking notes and a disjoint ranking reserve, but do not speak or display it in the individual flavor video, caption, recipe page, or public package copy.

## Weekly Planning

- Active cadence starting September 14, 2026: schedule five separated slots per day, or 35 slots per week. The fifth slot is a stretch target; publishing four strong, ready posts on a day is acceptable. Do not publish weak, duplicate, inaccurate, or unfinished work merely to fill slot five.
- Use the standard five-slot Pacific schedule: `8:30 AM`, `11:00 AM`, `1:30 PM`, `4:00 PM`, and `7:30 PM`. Keep posts at least 2-3 hours apart. Preserve the following week as a buffer for carryovers and unfinished anchors rather than filling it prematurely.
- Before changing a plan, inspect RECIPE VIDEO COVERAGE, WEEKLY CONTENT CHECK, TikTok posting history, and the two adjacent plan tabs. Never schedule an older unposted recipe behind a newer episode without an explicit reason.
- Treat replacing any required weekly anchor as a move, not a deletion. Before writing the replacement, choose and record a later slot for the displaced anchor in the same week, then re-run WEEKLY CONTENT CHECK and verify every required series still has exactly one valid scheduled row. Do not finalize the plan change while the displaced anchor is missing; use an excess carousel or other flexible slot when available.
- Publication gate: immediately before scheduling or moving any item, refresh the live TikTok profile and exact-match the title, series/episode, key caption language, and content premise against the post history. A local render marked `Ready` means only that the file exists; it never proves the post is unpublished. Mark verified matches as `Posted` with the TikTok URL in POSTING INVENTORY, then remove or replace the planned row.
- Do not use a stale performance tracker, a `Ready` label, or a missing RECIPE VIDEO COVERAGE checkmark as evidence that a video is unposted. Recipe coverage is a secondary check; live TikTok history is authoritative for recipes, community posts, product posts, and carousels alike.
- POSTING INVENTORY is the operational posting ledger. Buffer plans are a content backlog, not publication proof. At the beginning of each week, Tushar confirms changes from the scheduled list; update both the selected plan row and POSTING INVENTORY. Once an item goes live, log its actual date, platform URL, and `Posted` status before planning any replacement.
- Posting-ledger synchronization rule: every completed publishable package must create or update one planned row in POSTING INVENTORY with its canonical postable inventory ID. After every TikTok analytics refresh, reconcile every live TikTok post into POSTING INVENTORY: update a confidently matched planned row to `Posted`, record the actual Pacific posting date, canonical TikTok URL, and live-audit date, and preserve its schedule metadata. Add live posts with no confident planned-row match as separate `Posted` rows marked `Needs classification`; never force a fuzzy match. Flag past scheduled rows without a live match as `Audit needed - no live TikTok match`. TIKTOK LATEST is the raw replaceable snapshot; POSTING INVENTORY is the durable canonical ledger and must retain historical live posts.
- During every weekly audit, compare refreshed recipe/package slugs with posted versions. Surface every materially changed recipe as an `Updated version` candidate with its original URL/date, change summary, earliest eligible date, and current production status; do not let a generic recipe-level coverage check hide it. Minor corrections remain website-only and should not consume a posting slot.
- Treat Reelrise trend reports and similar creator suggestions as an idea and packaging input, not proof that a trend is currently performing. Save reusable concepts in the idea bank, verify time-sensitive trend claims before scheduling around them, and do not create new mandatory categories from a report unless Tushar explicitly adopts them.
- Reusable content lenses from the 2026-09-10 Reelrise report are: sensory/ASMR macro proof, Split Plate familiar-food remixes, sound-off macro hooks, authentic dinner-rescue stories, realistic lunchbox/family-food discussion, and stacked micro-payoff pacing. Apply them to suitable existing slots.
- Schedule exactly seven carousels per 35-slot week: one carousel on each day, never two carousels on the same day. The weekly mix is one dinner recipe carousel, three CrumblCreamiCut recipe carousels, two other Power-Up recipe carousels, and the Sunday X recap. Prefer prior-week Crumbl flavors so a flavor video and its carousel do not crowd the same day. Recipe selections remain Tushar-owned unless he names the recipe or explicitly asks Codex to select it.
- The Weekly Community Question is required. Keep `Tushar Topic TBD` only until Tushar supplies the idea, then replace the placeholder immediately. Also schedule one distinct seven-second text-thought or fact loop each week; it is separate from the recipe teaser and Sunday protein preview.
- Sunday X recap rule: publish one carousel every Sunday covering the account's actual X posts from the preceding Monday through Saturday. Use one cover followed by one card per post, in chronological order, with a maximum of six post cards. Preserve each post's meaning, date, and source URL; use fewer cards when no post exists for a day, and never invent a missing post or engagement metric. This recap replaces one rotating non-dinner recipe carousel, so the weekly carousel mix is exactly one dinner recipe carousel, one rotating cookbook/non-dinner recipe carousel, and one X recap carousel. Use a discussion caption that asks which thought should become a video or which one resonated most.
- Sunday `Protein for Dinner This Week` rule: publish one short video every Sunday showing the proteins actually purchased or prepared for the coming week's dinners and naming each confirmed dish. Keep unrevealed `Viral Recipes Proteinized` ingredients or concepts explicitly labeled as a surprise; do not leak the topic. This is a preview, not a recipe tutorial, so do not claim dish macros unless the exact complete serving has already been confirmed. Default to 10-25 seconds, readable package/action footage, original sound beneath concise Marcus narration, and the closing question `Which dinner do you want to see first?`. It does not replace the seven-second text-thought slot in the 35-slot cadence.
- SplitPlate Comedy is a required placeholder in the 35-slot plan, but not an automatic production approval. Keep it as `Topic pending - Tushar approval required` until Tushar supplies or approves the premise. Do not revive or post an older retired comedy edit merely to fill the slot.
- Portfolio target across the rolling 14-day buffer: Reach 25%, Growth 25%, Community 30%, Monetization 20%. Community includes personal yapping, nutrition, workouts, kids, household systems, goals, and honest hot takes. Do not label a recipe video as Monetization merely because it names a product.
- Weekly anchors for the 35-slot cadence: five CrumblCreamiCut videos (prior-week ranking, teaser, and three flavors); seven carousels (one dinner, three CrumblCreamiCut, two other Power-Ups, and Sunday X recap); one Viral Recipes Proteinized; one Proffee; one Can I Make It Viral attempt; one Snack Box; one dinner teaser; one Split Plate family-dinner main; one adult dinner follow-up about 14 days after its main; one under-15 comment recipe 7-14 days after its main; one Sunday `Protein for Dinner This Week`; one Quick Lunch; one Sauce Series; one What I Eat; one workout; one SplitPlate Comedy placeholder; one Weekly Community Question; one seven-second text-thought or fact loop; one genuine-use product post; one non-Crumbl ranking build; and one `Five Things Worth...` build. Fill the four remaining flexible video slots from verified ready-unposted inventory while protecting the Reach/Growth/Community/Monetization mix.
- The weekly calendar must contain 28 videos and seven carousels. Count formats and required anchors after every edit. A similar post does not satisfy a named anchor, and a placeholder is not `Ready`.
- Keep related pieces separated. No recipe component, parent meal, follow-up carousel, product tie-in, or adult cut should crowd the same seven-day window unless it is the scheduled CrumblCreamiCut sequence. Hold a recipe carousel until at least seven days after its main and an adult dinner cut until about day 14.
- Every plan review must record honest checklist gaps rather than mark a requirement complete because a similar video exists elsewhere.
- Audience-fit planning gate: at least 60% of posts in each rolling 14-day buffer should contain an explicit parent, family-system, work-life, lunchbox, household, or fitness-parenting signal; every remaining post must still be compatible with the target audience. Do not schedule a full day of three or more generic food posts with no life-stage signal.
- Record the intended audience signal in the plan notes for every newly built post. Use flexible Community, Quick Lunch, Snack Box, workout, What I Eat, dinner, and product slots to repair weak audience coverage without changing the required weekly category count.
- Geographic timing is a test, not a targeting guarantee. Keep using morning, midday, and evening Pacific slots to expose different English-speaking time zones, then adjust from actual follower activity and country-level performance rather than assumptions.
