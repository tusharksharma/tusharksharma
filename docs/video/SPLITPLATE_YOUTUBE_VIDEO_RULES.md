# The Split Plate YouTube Video Rules

Use this for landscape, long-form YouTube recipe videos. These rules are separate from TikTok, Reels, and YouTube Shorts rules.

## Format And Source Footage

- Build every long-form YouTube video in a `1920x1080`, `16:9` landscape project.
- Existing portrait footage is valid source material. Place `1080x1920` clips inside the landscape frame and scale them to fit the full height by default. Changing the project aspect ratio cannot recover scenery outside the original portrait recording.
- Use clean source clips without baked-in captions or music whenever available. Keep the original frame rate where practical.
- Do not stretch portrait footage or fill the landscape canvas by default. A full-frame crop removes roughly 68% of the portrait picture; use it only for a brief close-up when the complete action remains visible and the enlarged image stays acceptably sharp.
- Unless the user gives clip-specific direction, use `Saturation +10`, `Exposure -5`, `Brilliance +6`, and `Sharpen +6` as the default starting grade. Do not stack it onto footage that is already corrected or a prior graded export, and reduce individual adjustments when the source would clip, amplify noise, or make skin or food look artificial.

## Default Landscape Layout

- Default to `portrait video + useful notes` as the main Split Plate layout.
- Keep the portrait clip sharp and use the extra horizontal space for information that advances the recipe: ingredients, method checkpoints, macro context, substitutions, what can go wrong, fixes, family split, or verdict.
- Do not fill the side panel with decorative filler, repeated captions, or information unrelated to the action currently shown.
- Use a blurred duplicate background only when a useful side panel would add clutter or there is no meaningful supporting information. Keep the sharp portrait source clearly dominant.
- Use two portrait clips side by side for adult/kid plates, before/after results, flavor comparisons, successful/failed attempts, or two simultaneous method details.
- Use selective landscape crops for tight food actions such as scoops, pours, sizzles, cuts, texture reveals, and plated-food beauty shots.
- Future shoots should add a few native horizontal clips: finished dish, introduction, key technique, verdict, and any comparison that benefits from width. Do not require every recipe step to be refilmed horizontally.

## Long-Form Recipe Structure

- The YouTube video is a teaching expansion of the recipe page, not a stretched short-form edit.
- Start with the finished result and a concise promise: what the dish is, who it helps, and the practical or macro payoff.
- Use the canonical recipe page as the factual source for ingredients, quantities, method, serving count, macros, substitutions, and food-safety details. Apply the user's latest corrections over older page copy and update the website handoff when they conflict.
- Explain the method in enough detail that a viewer can understand why each consequential step works. Do not merely read the ingredient list.
- Include a dedicated `what can go wrong` section when useful. Cover likely texture, timing, temperature, substitution, portioning, storage, or reheating failures and give specific fixes grounded in the tested recipe.
- Add relevant supplemental clips from the inventory when they improve understanding: ingredient packages, alternate angles, prior attempts, component recipes, family plates, meal-prep storage, reheating, or a later taste result.
- Keep supplemental footage truthful and clearly connected to the current explanation. Do not imply that footage from another batch is the exact batch currently being prepared.
- Use continuous narration to connect portrait footage, side-panel notes, close-ups, and comparisons. Let important sensory moments breathe under original sound.
- End with the finished dish, an honest verdict, the most important takeaway, and the natural next action: recipe page, related component, or relevant question.

## Reuse And Overlap Policy

- Treat YouTube long-form as a separate format from TikTok, Reels, and YouTube Shorts. Source footage may overlap across those platforms.
- Do not apply the short-form zero-source-overlap rule to long-form YouTube videos. Reusing the best recipe actions is allowed because the landscape teaching edit has a different viewing context and purpose.
- The YouTube result must still be materially distinct: landscape composition, longer teaching narrative, recipe-page detail, troubleshooting, supplemental footage, and a different opening and payoff structure.
- Do not upload an unchanged short-form vertical export as the long-form YouTube video. YouTube Shorts remain governed by `SPLITPLATE_TIKTOK_EDIT_RULES.md`.
- Treat the belief that cross-platform source reuse will not be penalized as an operating assumption, not a guaranteed algorithm rule. Judge performance from YouTube analytics and revise when evidence changes.

## Package And Inventory

- Every completed long-form package should include the `1920x1080` posting master, clean master when useful, narration script, chapter outline, thumbnail brief or asset, recipe-page source URL, claims and macro audit, supplemental-source manifest, and technical QA.
- Inventory the long-form export separately from its short-form family. Link related recipe and short-form inventory IDs without marking them as duplicates.
- Record which footage is reused from short-form and which clips are new or supplemental. Reuse is allowed, but provenance must remain traceable.
- Keep all on-screen text inside YouTube title-safe areas and verify the final video on desktop and mobile playback before delivery.
