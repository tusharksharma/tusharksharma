# Local video storage

Confirmed by Tushar on October 18, 2026: clean up unnecessary local files as production proceeds when disk space becomes tight. Footage is kept in Google Photos or Drive; local working downloads do not need to accumulate indefinitely. Routine cleanup within this scope does not require asking again.

- Check free space before large downloads, proxy generation and renders. Reclaim enough space for the next operation, rather than deleting broadly.
- Start with stopped failed-render scratch files, superseded test exports, disposable QA frames, unused proxies and regenerable intermediates. Confirm that the active editor, render, upload and current approved timeline no longer depend on them. Use HyperFrames' managed cleanup with a dry run for its caches; preserve project history.
- Before removing downloaded originals or finished media, verify that the corresponding recoverable copy is actually available in Google Photos or Drive. Record the cloud location and identifying metadata in the project; retain original-quality files when exact originals are needed. Do not assume that newly created exports or audio were backed up merely because the camera footage was.
- Keep the current master and editable project while review/upload is pending. Preserve scripts, edit/source maps, captions, thumbnail, approvals, paid narration takes, credit receipts and upload records until their recoverable copies are verified. Paid takes are not disposable caches.
- Keep reusable reaction photos, intro/music and subscriber graphics available to future agents or verify their cloud source before removing a local duplicate.
- Limit deletion to resolved, explicitly checked project/cache paths. Do not remove unrelated Downloads, Photos/Drive library items, or the last recoverable copy. Record material cleanup and freed space in the project.

This is conditional space management, not a request to purge files when there is ample free space. It does not change video publication or sharing authorization.
