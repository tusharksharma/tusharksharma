## Core Positioning

- **Audience target:** English-speaking parents ages 25-45, primarily in the United States and also Australia, the United Kingdom, Canada, and similar developed English-speaking markets.
- The primary account promise is **high-protein food that fits family life**. `One cook, two plates` is the clearest dinner expression of that promise. Practical lunches, shortcuts, desserts, Creamis, products, fitness, and community posts remain valid when their relationship to real family life is truthful.
- Before approving a narrative, identify the post's primary audience signal: family dinner, lunchbox, bedtime, workday pressure, different adult/kid preferences, grocery reality, home-gym access, or another concrete parent system. Do not rely on a generic closing question to establish the audience after the recipe is over.
- Do not force a parent label or hook into every post. Creami and dessert videos may lead with flavor, texture, a surprising ingredient, an experiment, or a useful discovery when that is the strongest concept.
- Avoid `zero-guilt`, `guilt-free reward`, cheat-food morality, and similar framing. Enjoyment without food guilt is the house voice.
- **Dinner completion gate:** allocate footage and produce the companion cuts together. Every dinner requires a distinct (1) 7-second teaser, (2) full Split Plate version, and (3) full adult version. A fourth, under-15-second comment-recipe cut is required when the complete served recipe has six or more ingredients (excluding water and optional garnish), or when Tushar explicitly requests it. Do not call the package complete until every required cut is rendered, packaged, inventoried separately, and audited for zero cross-cut raw source-time overlap. Do not silently omit a required cut because an earlier edit consumed its footage.
- Dinner videos should sell the "One Cook, Two Plates" system, not just the food.
- **Split Cook Method™ logo cue:** When narration says “Split Cook Method” (or an unnarrated edit introduces that exact method), show the approved transparent logo from `/Users/tusharsharma/Documents/New project/video-edits/hyperframes-components/split-cook-method-logo.svg` timed to the phrase. Animate one controlled bounce, then clear it; do not use it as a bumper, permanent watermark, or substitute for showing the two plates. Keep the mark in the established warm-black/lime/coral palette and within the mobile safe area.
- For dinner recipes, show the adult plate and kid plate early when footage exists.
- A single adult plate can be the payoff, but the split adult/kid composition is the stronger brand hook.
- For every dinner with enough footage, build two full-length publishable edits: a Split Plate version that explains the shared cook and the adult/kid finishes, plus an adult-focused companion that follows the adult recipe, macros, and payoff without prescribing kid portions. Give the two edits different visual spines and use alternate source ranges wherever possible.
- Create two separate 1080x1920 manual-cover assets for every dinner: one adult cover centered on the finished adult plate with adult macros, and one Split Plate cover that visibly includes both plate styles and names the shared-cook value. Covers are standalone image files for manual upload and are not baked into the videos.
- Use practical parent language: weeknight hack, busy parents, one pan, one cook, kid-friendly version.
- Core origin story: the user cooked separately for kids for about 6 years. Kids ate around 5:30 pm, adults ate around 8 pm after bedtime, and dinner meant up to 3 hours in the kitchen. When the kids reached roughly 3 and 6, the family bought a new dining table and intentionally moved toward eating together with one cook and two plate styles.
- Split-plate philosophy: kids are not given keto/fat-free macro substitutes by default. Kids should generally get normal growing-years food; their adjustments are usually deconstructed plates, less spice, smaller portions, normal tortillas/bagels/cheese, or simpler sauce.
- Do not prescribe exact kid calories, macros, or portions in social videos unless the user explicitly asks. State the creator's adult target when relevant, then tell parents to build each child's plate around that child's appetite and needs.
- Weekly hot-take/discussion videos can use this origin story to build personal connection. Keep them food-adjacent and practical, not random rant content.

## Seven Engagement Principles (Every Video, Including No-Marcus Cuts)

These are a creative QA pass, not permission to invent facts, shame food choices, or force controversy. Use the strongest applicable expression of each principle and record it in the cut plan. A silent video must carry the story through real action, sound, and readable text.

1. **Have a strong opinion.** State a specific, defensible take instead of a neutral topic label. Mark taste as personal preference and nutrition comparisons with their serving basis.
2. **Talk to one person.** Write to a recognisable viewer in one situation (for example, a parent in the drive-through who also cares about protein), not to a vague crowd.
3. **Name what that person feels.** Identify the honest tension—rushed, hungry, bored of the default, or tired of making a second meal. Show or say only what the footage and audience context support; do not claim to know everyone's emotions.
4. **Say the useful thing they won't.** Voice a plain, slightly uncomfortable observation that helps them choose, without shaming them or claiming one food is morally better.
5. **Use their words.** Favor the audience's everyday phrasing (`same calories`, `my usual order`, `what would you pick?`) over creator jargon such as `protein efficiency`.
6. **Be clear about what you are against.** Challenge a habit or assumption, not a person or food. For a comparison, oppose automatic ordering or misleading framing—not nuggets, children, or pleasure.
7. **Control pacing.** Open on the strongest real sensory action; land the point in the first three seconds; change the visual or information when attention would dip; leave enough time to read the payoff; finish with one natural engagement prompt. Do not stretch a short idea to meet a duration target.

Before preview approval, check all seven against the actual timeline. In no-Marcus cuts, the exact text, shot, and original sound must perform these jobs without explanatory narration. Pair bold claims with a source/date check, and put qualifications where the claim is read—not only in the caption.

## First 3 Seconds

- Never open with a static finished plate, website/tablet/card footage, or anything that looks like a still image.
- Open with action: sauce pour, sprinkle, crunch, pan sizzle, cheese pull, hand rotating plate, bite/tear, or food entering frame.
- **Action-first pattern-interrupt rule:** audition the raw footage for a prominent, genuine event in the first second—a crunch bite, cheese pull, splash, scoop drop, utensil slip, or other tactile surprise. Do not automatically trim a harmless unplanned moment because it looks imperfect; it may be the reason a viewer pauses. Make the event readable at phone size, keep its real sound when useful, and connect it immediately to the video's comparison, question, or payoff. Never stage a fake accident or imply a safety failure for engagement.
- Macros and labels explain the hook; they are not a substitute for the visual event. Let the action land before dense text competes with it, then deliver the claim/context within the opening 1-3 seconds. A hook should still be understandable when muted.
- When available, combine three complementary hook layers: (1) moving sensory proof, (2) a short sound-off overlay carrying the macro, time, texture, or practical promise, and (3) spoken context or curiosity that does not repeat the overlay word for word. Two strong layers are better than forcing weak narration or unnecessary text.
- For parent-led formats, make the life-stage signal visible or audible in the opening three seconds. Useful structures include `ONE COOK = TWO PLATES`, `AFTER BEDTIME`, `SCHOOL-LUNCH REALITY`, `DINNER BEFORE MY 11 PM EDIT SHIFT`, or a specific real situation supplied by Tushar. Do not use a parent label when the footage and story do not support it.
- For dinner videos, use this pattern when possible:
  - 0.0-1.5s: sensory action hook.
  - 1.5-3.0s: flash-forward to adult + kid plates.
  - Text: `1 PAN = 2 DINNERS`, `1 COOK = 2 PLATES`, or similar.
- Verify the flash frame actually shows both adult and kid plates; do not label it split-plate if only one plate is visible.

## Pacing

- Do not force every video to 30 or 60 seconds.
- Simple snack/power-up videos can be 10-30 seconds.
- Simple dinners often work around 45-60 seconds.
- More involved dinners can be 60-90 seconds if the sequence needs context.
- When Reelrise recommends `Magic Cut`, treat it as step-level selection: keep one clear action beat per necessary step, remove duplicate angles and repeated mechanics, then preserve enough room for a natural explanation. Do not make the result feel frantic merely to hit a shorter duration.
- Cut repetitive prep aggressively: long stirring, scraping, seasoning, chopping, and empty-pan footage.
- Speed up mechanical steps, usually 1.5x-2.5x.
- Keep or slow down high-value sensory moments: pour, crunch, sizzle, flip, sauce drizzle, plating reveal, final split plates.
- For ASMR or original-sound-only recipes, do not simulate pacing by heavily accelerating every surviving action. Use real-time magic cuts around the useful part of each pour, scoop, crush, rim, blend, and garnish; remove setup and dead air instead. Ingredient labels should usually remain readable for about 2.5-4 seconds. Reserve speed changes for genuinely repetitive mechanics, and keep them subtle enough that the natural sound still feels credible.
- Dinner videos should feel quick but not frantic.
- In edits longer than about 15 seconds, aim for a meaningful visual change or micro-payoff roughly every 6-10 seconds: transformation, sauce reveal, texture proof, adult/kid split, plating, or macro payoff. This is a retention check, not a rigid timer.
- Use 1.5-2.5 second action beats for simple readable mechanics when the footage supports them. Keep longer holds for finished-food inspection, family context, important explanation, and satisfying original-sound moments.
- Sync a cut to an audible chop, pour, plate drop, crack, or sizzle when it improves clarity. Do not bake in trending music merely to manufacture beat-sync; Tushar normally adds current platform audio in-app.
- If a sauce, drizzle, crema, or dip is the differentiator, give it a real visual chapter. Show the key ingredients entering the bowl in order, then the mix/pour/spread, instead of compressing it into one generic sauce shot.
- Keep side dishes out of the middle of the main build unless the side is the hook. For burger/sandwich videos, show fries/chips after the burger or sandwich is assembled so they read as plating, not a narrative interruption.

## Default Footage Grade

- Preserve the source footage's original colors by default. Do not apply a routine saturation, exposure, brilliance, sharpening, LUT, or "food pop" grade. The earlier `Saturation +10`, `Exposure -5`, `Brilliance +6`, `Sharpen +6` preset is retired as a default; use adjustments only when Tushar asks for them or a specific clip demonstrably needs a technical correction.
- If a technical correction is warranted, inspect skin tone, food color, highlights, shadows, and compression against the original. Reduce or remove an adjustment when it clips, looks artificial, or exaggerates noise.
- Apply any justified correction to the footage, not generated hook imagery. Preserve the authentic appearance of the food and room.
- Preflight every iPhone source for HDR metadata before grading. HyperFrames supports HDR, so preserve native HLG/BT.2020 footage through the composition and render when HDR delivery is intended; do not convert to SDR merely because the source is iPhone HDR.
- Choose HDR or SDR delivery deliberately for the target platform and archive that decision in the project notes. Use SDR only when compatibility, a specific publishing workflow, or an approved cross-platform master requires it.
- When SDR delivery is required, use a deliberate HLG/BT.2020-to-Rec.709 tone map and explicitly tag the working master and output. Do not rely on an implicit conversion, which can flatten saturation and make the finished video look pale.
- Compare representative source, working, and encoded frames before approval. Preserve the established warm black/lime/coral overlay palette, and correct the footage pipeline rather than changing brand colors to compensate for pale video.
- Never stack the standard grade blindly on top of an HDR-to-SDR tone map. Tune the conversion first, then add only the smallest extra adjustment needed for believable food, skin, wood, greens, and package colors.

## Render Efficiency And Cost Guard

- Use one approved visual master per timeline. Marcus, text/original-sound, clean-audio, and other sound-only variants reuse that exact video stream; create them by muxing audio, not by rerendering the footage and graphics.
- For iPhone HDR plus designed overlays, the default production path is: one clean graded HLG base, one HyperFrames ProRes 4444 graphics-alpha render, one final composite, then audio remuxes. Use a fully layered HDR render only when an effect cannot be separated from the footage.
- Before a long render, encode short proofs through the actual final path for the hook, a representative transition, the CTA entrance, and the end frame. Inspect extracted encoded frames for color, stale layers, safe zones, legibility, timing, and coverage. Studio playback and browser snapshots are useful diagnostics but are not final-render evidence.
- Never launch a second full-length HDR render merely because the first one failed. Reproduce the problem in a `3-8s` encoded proof, correct it, and pass that proof before another long pass.
- Stop and switch to the alpha-composite path when a render estimates over roughly `10 GB` of temporary frames, is likely to take more than about `8 minutes`, or would become a second unproven full HDR pass.
- Cache source transcodes, graded bases, alpha overlays, voice stems, and original-audio stems for the duration of the package. A copy, cover, or audio revision should touch only the affected layer.
- The normal compute budget is one expensive full-length visual pass. If the build needs more, record the reason in the project notes and prove the change with a short encoded test first.

## Multi-Version Recipe And Reserve Workflow

- For every dinner, decide the required three-cut baseline and whether the complexity rule adds a fourth cut **before editing any timeline**. Allocate all required cuts and the reserve bank from the same raw-footage review. Different posting dates do not turn this into separate production jobs.
- For other complicated recipes, plan purpose-specific companion cuts only when enough distinct preparation, transformation, or payoff footage exists to support genuinely different posts; the six-ingredient comment-cut rule still applies across recipe series.
- For **non-dinner** simple assembly recipes, snack boxes, quick sandwiches, basic drinks, or other builds whose complete method is understandable in roughly 15-30 seconds, do not automatically require a teaser or `comment recipe` cut. Prioritize one strong main edit and preserve alternate footage for compilations, community posts, or future series recaps. The dinner three-cut baseline still applies to dinners.
- Decide the scope before allocating footage. If an optional short version would merely repeat the main edit without creating a new curiosity hook or useful compressed recipe story, keep it as an internal compilation asset rather than scheduling it as a standalone post. For a required dinner cut, request pickup footage or explicit scope approval rather than silently skipping it.
- For dinner packages, allocate the strongest raw moments across these distinct jobs before building any one edit:
  - **7-second teaser:** its own immediate sensory/finished-dish hook and curiosity gap, not a trimmed full edit or a compressed tutorial.
  - **Full Split Plate:** the shared cook, adult and kid plate finishes, practical family reason, naturally paced Marcus narration, and engagement question.
  - **Full adult:** a distinct adult-food visual spine, method, and payoff, without reusing the Split Plate cut's raw source-time ranges.
  - **Under-15 comment recipe, when required:** fresh magic-cut result and minimum readable method, minimal Marcus narration, adult macros when confirmed, and `Comment "recipe" and I'll send it`. A 9-12-second result is preferred when understandable.
  - **Community reserve:** about five strong, clearly labeled unused clips or source ranges for later hot takes, rankings, compilations, comment replies, and recreation videos.
- Do not build a companion cut by trimming another cut. Source-time ownership is exclusive: the Split Plate full, adult full, teaser, required comment cut, and reserve bank must not share any raw source-time range. Clean, text-overlay, and Marcus exports of the exact same timeline count as one variant and are the only exemption.
- Allocate the strongest footage deliberately:
  - Split Plate main gets the clearest shared-cook process and both plate finishes.
  - Adult main gets its own complete adult-focused process and finished-food payoff.
  - Teaser gets one highly legible sensory action that can loop without exposing the whole recipe.
  - Required comment cut gets fresh one-second major actions that communicate the recipe quickly.
  - Reserve gets alternate angles, unused reactions, ingredient reveals, family plates, and five-to-ten-second continuous actions that can support future narration.
- Keep the short discovery version visually complete through magic cuts: finished-food action, two or three defining build beats, split/payoff when relevant, macros, then the comment CTA.
- Create and retain a source manifest for every publishable edit and the reserve bank. Record the filename, source start/end times, purpose, and which output owns the range. Run an exact overlap audit before final packaging; the completion gate is zero cross-variant overlap and zero repeated source range inside each publishable timeline.
- Do not delete or repurpose the five reserve clips during routine cleanup unless the user explicitly releases them.
- Treat the `UNUSED_CLIP_INVENTORY` tab in the Video Inventory workbook as the canonical cross-project reserve index. Every actual trimmed reserve clip must receive a stable `UC-####` ID and a row containing its recipe or series, source file and in/out range, saved path, visual description, shot type, orientation, audio usefulness, status, and intended reuse.
- Updating `UNUSED_CLIP_INVENTORY` is a hard completion gate for every new raw-video build, including companion versions and refreshes. Read the tab **before** source-range selection; add newly exported reserve clips before `Ready` or delivery; read the written rows back to verify ID, source in/out, path, and status. Keep a package-level allocation map covering Split Plate, adult, teaser, required comment cut, and reserves, and record the no-overlap audit in each reserve row's notes. Do not add vague ideas, theoretical clips, or unaudited timestamps. If the source has no safe reserve, document the shortfall and obtain Tushar's explicit exception before treating the package as complete.
- Reserve status lifecycle is `reserved` -> `planned` -> `used`; use `retired` only when the clip is intentionally withdrawn. Never delete a used row: record the published post or version in `used_in` so future edits can avoid accidental reuse.
- If raw footage cannot support all required distinct edits and useful reserves, identify the exact missing shot types and ask for pickup footage or explicit approval to reduce scope. Keep the package marked incomplete until that decision; do not reuse footage to fill the gap. Reduce the reserve bank before dropping a required publishable cut.
- When rebuilding a post that received no distribution, treat every raw range used in all previously posted versions as unavailable. Changing only the hook, CTA, duration, or one clip is not a materially new edit when the rest of the visual timeline is unchanged.
- Before delivering a replacement, compare its source manifest against every prior posted manifest and verify that the new visual spine has no reused raw ranges whenever enough untouched footage exists.
- The standing Split Plate music-library preference permits a fitting, softly mixed original bed in the first preview; select and rotate by story/pacing rather than automatically using one track. Preserve original cooking sound beneath Marcus and any strong sensory hook. If music weakens the action or narration, leave it out. Finalize a baked-in bed only with the approved cut; see `SPLITPLATE_PRODUCTION_RULES.md` and the library README for selection and mix QA.
- Inventory each required dinner cut and reserve asset separately. Give all publishable versions different filenames, captions, and posting slots. Every rendered canonical MP4 gets its own `VIDEO_INVENTORY` row: Split Plate, adult, teaser, and the under-15 comment recipe when required, plus any retained clean/text/Marcus variants. Do not let an output disappear merely because another row represents the same recipe.
- **Agenda/inventory readback gate:** when a video was built for a scheduled agenda item, delivery is not complete until the exact agenda row contains the final inventory ID, current status, postable path, and production note, and the matching `VIDEO_INVENTORY` row points to the approved canonical file and current hash. Read both rows back after writing and verify the canonical path still exists. A successful render by itself is not completion.
- Inventory optional short cuts from simple recipes as `compilation reserve` unless Tushar explicitly approves them for standalone posting. Do not assign them a posting slot merely because they were rendered.
- Treat the captions as distinct jobs, not shorter copies of one another:
  - Split Plate caption = the approved shared-cook/family story, practical value, macros with serving basis, attribution, and discussion prompt.
  - Adult caption = the adult meal and its own food/macro payoff, without pretending the kid plate is shown.
  - Teaser caption = a concise curiosity gap based on the teaser's unique footage; no complete method and no `comment recipe` CTA.
  - Required comment-cut caption = result/macros plus a compact recipe promise and `Comment "recipe" and I'll send it.`
  - Use a distinct, search-relevant hashtag set for each publishing purpose. A clean internal master may retain the main caption metadata.

### Channel Introductions And Cross-Recipe Montages

- Build pinned channel introductions, brand manifestos, community montages, and cross-recipe roundups from **unused footage only**. Previously published source ranges are unavailable even when the new narration, crop, duration, or overlays are different.
- Use this source priority: newly recorded pickup shots, clips marked `reserved` in a recipe's `broll-bank/manifest.json`, then verified-unused raw ranges. Never pull a convenient shot directly from a finished published video.
- Before rendering, create a source-use manifest that records each clip's source file, in/out range, reserve manifest when applicable, and prior-use audit result.
- After publishing, mark every selected reserve clip `used` and name the channel-introduction or montage version that consumed it.
- If there is not enough verified-unused footage to support a visually varied edit, request specific pickup shots and wait. Do not fill the gaps with repeated recipe footage that could suppress distribution or make the channel introduction feel recycled.

## Text Overlays

- Keep text short, TikTok-friendly, and usually top-middle.
- Treat TikTok's top search/header area and bottom caption area as hard exclusion zones for critical text. At any vertical resolution, keep essential copy below the top 8% and above the bottom 16% of the frame. At `1080x1920`, this means no essential text above approximately `y=160` or below approximately `y=1610` (about 310 px reserved at the bottom).
- Hooks, macros, ingredient quantities, subtitles, disclaimers, and CTAs must all stay inside that vertical safe area. Footage may extend edge to edge; only nonessential decoration may enter the excluded zones.
- Every new Marcus-narrated posting edit includes embedded, speech-synced captions by default. Use the final paced audio for timing, proofread names/claims against the approved script, keep short readable phrase groups, and check that captions do not collide with hooks or CTAs. Caption presence and safe-area playback are required export QA; a clean/uncaptioned master may remain an internal companion, not the default posting file.
- Include safe-zone compliance in the final contact-sheet and playback QA. Do not approve a video because text is technically visible in the raw MP4 if TikTok controls or captions will cover it in-app.
- Text should explain the hook, macros, key brands, or split-plate logic; it should not cover the food for too long.
- The opening overlay should usually communicate one promise only: macros, time, transformation, texture, or family-system value. Keep it readable for muted viewers and let the voiceover provide a different but complementary reason to stay.
- Do not use fear-based or parent-shaming hooks such as `Stop feeding your kids this`. Do not manufacture conflict with teachers, children, or normal foods for engagement.
- End recipe videos by saying `Visit thesplitplate.com` and showing `thesplitplate.com` on screen. Do not use `link in bio` unless the user explicitly requests it for that post.
- Good overlay types:
  - hook + macros: `STEAK + FRIES / 620 CAL | 45G PROTEIN`
  - split concept: `1 PAN = 2 DINNERS / adult + kid plate`
  - practical reason: `NO-JUDGE CHIMICHURRI / weeknight parent hack`
  - brand/product hook: `SOLA BAGEL`, `FAT FREE CHEDDAR`, `WILDE CHIPS`
  - final engagement: `What's your go-to protein for picky eaters?`

## Captions And Hashtags

- When building a TikTok/Reels/Shorts edit from Reelrise feedback, include a caption and hashtag set in the final handoff unless the user explicitly asks for video only.
- Captions should start with the hook or hot take, then give the practical value, then end with a low-friction comment prompt when useful.
- Keep captions conversational and parent-aware. Avoid sounding like a recipe blog excerpt.
- When the post is parent-led, put the life-stage signal in the first or second sentence rather than relying only on `#BusyParents`. For food-first posts, use a truthful parent use case only when it improves the premise.
- Use clear international English. Prefer searchable concepts such as `family dinner`, `school lunch`, `quick lunch`, `one cook two plates`, `home gym`, and `weeknight meal`; do not depend on US-only brand names or local slang to explain the post.
- Use `link in bio` language only when pointing viewers to the full recipe; do not paste the website URL in the caption unless the user asks.
- Hashtags should be a tight mix of:
  - 2-3 recipe/category tags, e.g. `#HighProteinDinner`, `#ChickenQuesadilla`, `#ProteinDessert`
  - 1-2 audience/niche tags, e.g. `#FamilyDinner`, `#BusyParents`, `#MacroFriendlyMeals`
  - 1-2 trend/search tags, e.g. `#MealPrep`, `#EasyDinner`, `#TikTokFood`
- Do not use `#TheSplitPlate` as a default inventory hashtag. It is a brand label, not a useful discovery/category signal. Replace it with the most specific truthful niche or search tag for that post, such as `#HighProteinMeals`, `#FamilyMealIdeas`, `#MacroFriendlyRecipes`, `#ProteinDessert`, or `#BusyParentMeals`.
- Every inventory hashtag must answer a discovery job: recipe type, nutrition/use case, audience, or active search category. Avoid redundant tags that categorize the account but not the post.
- Avoid oversized hashtag blocks. Usually 5-8 hashtags is enough.
- If Reelrise suggests a debate angle, turn it into the caption question, e.g. `Is warm cereal still cereal or breakfast soup?`
- New TikTok caption pattern from the latest three posts:
  - Start with the strongest plain-English hook in the first sentence. Examples: `200 cal, 22g protein per sandwich. 5 minutes. No cooking.`, `Stop using regular milk! Upgrade your cereal with a 30g protein shake instead.`, `The easiest way to cut a watermelon for quick summer snacking.`
  - Use 4-5 searchable hashtags, not a large block.
  - For quick hacks, keep the caption short: hook + practical benefit + question + hashtags.
  - For recipe posts, include a compact recipe block after the hook/hashtags only when useful: macros, time/servings, ingredients, and short numbered method.
  - The spreadsheet `hashtags` field should mirror the final hashtag set without `#` symbols.

## Voiceover

- Always draft the speech and get user approval before generating or adding narration.
- Every Marcus video needs one primary closing CTA that fits the post. For standard-length recipe, sauce, hack, and food-tip videos, the approved asparagus pattern also places an earned save trigger at the reusable method, a specific share trigger at the visual payoff, and a comment question at the end when the footage supports all three. Separate them in time and purpose; do not read a list of commands. A recipe video over 40 seconds may add a conversational mid-roll like ask only when it does not crowd those beats. Micro-cuts use one clear ask.
- Use the native, centered translucent-glass bookmark/save capsule at the save-worthy food beat; it need not occupy the final 3 seconds if a genuine comment question is the primary close. Give a share trigger its own small, translucent cue. Keep moving food, serving, tasting, or creator payoff visible through and around both.
- Do not default to raster sticker GIFs, white sticker backgrounds, cartoon pointing arms, finger/elbow animations, or multiple competing CTA decorations. When Tushar says `save GIF`, interpret the intent as a clearly animated save affordance; use the approved glass treatment unless he explicitly requests a literal GIF asset.
- On a `1080x1920` frame, use the approved dumpling-video treatment as the reference: roughly `620px` wide, centered horizontally near the middle of the safe frame, dark translucent fill around 45-58% opacity, subtle background blur, thin light border, lime bookmark accent, and concise copy such as `SAVE THIS` plus one short context line. Adjust placement to preserve the focal food or face.
- Prefer native HTML/SVG for CTA graphics because it stays crisp and deterministic in render. If a literal GIF is explicitly required, pre-size the source asset to its intended pixel dimensions and position it through a persistent wrapper rather than relying only on child-image CSS.
