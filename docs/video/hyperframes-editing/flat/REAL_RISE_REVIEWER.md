## Review procedure

1. Identify the exact video, version, approved script, recipe, macro basis, and source files. Record the objective: stronger hook, less idle footage, clearer method, or another specific problem.
   Match feedback timestamps to that exact version. Screenshots may mix original and revised cuts; do not apply an old timestamp to the new timeline or repeat a change already solved.
2. Inspect the existing timeline and raw footage. A recommendation that a pour, blend, or reaction is missing may mean it was omitted from the edit—not that it was never filmed. Use contact sheets to locate it, then inspect the exact source range.
3. Sort suggestions into **accept**, **adapt**, **reject**, and **future shoot**. Give a concrete reason for each consequential choice. Do not invent an action or sound to satisfy the review.
4. Propose the new hook, narrative, exact Marcus read, visual changes, and closing question. Keep the recipe understandable; shortening must not erase necessary steps.
5. Build the authorized version, inspect the preview, then verify the encoded export before marking it ready. Save the decision record and add a learning entry below.

## Creative checks

- **Start with real motion.** Put the strongest genuine pour, scoop, crunch, cheese pull, or reveal on the opening frame. Remove setup and empty vessels. A headline or macro card cannot rescue an idle hero.
- **Make the tension specific.** “Butter on protein ice cream? Hear me out” is grounded in this build. Do not automatically adopt generic “Stop scrolling” language or manufacture controversy.
- **Use the seven principles.** Have a strong opinion; talk to one person; name their real feeling; say the useful thing they may not say; use their words; challenge a habit or assumption rather than a person; control pacing. Apply them where the story earns them, not as seven forced lines.
- **Support the hero with macros.** When verified, show calories and protein together near the opening and state both early. Identify the serving basis and estimates. Let the moving food remain the main visual.
- **Keep the proof visible.** Match ingredient, transformation, texture, and taste lines to the actual corresponding action. Semantic synchronization matters more than making every narration waveform peak trigger a cut.
- **Remove dead time, not useful explanation.** Trim parked cups, repeated stirring, late entrances, and empty-kitchen tails. Check the entire selected range, not just its first frame. Do not force a universal duration or frantic cutting.
- **Give Marcus an emotional arc.** Use the required ElevenLabs v4 Marcus with appropriate curiosity, playfulness, conversational explanation, and earned delight. Verify the actual generated model and performance; tags alone do not prove expressive delivery.
- **Keep text subordinate.** Use short switching captions, at most two lines, and compact local translucent cards. Avoid simultaneously competing headlines, ratings, macro cards, ingredient lists, and CTAs. Check phone readability and avoid covering eyes, hands, or the food payoff.
- **Close with one primary action.** Use a specific natural question or other approved CTA. A small contextual save cue can appear earlier when the method earns it. Do not stack spoken save, send, follow, and comment commands at the end.
- **Respect the thumbnail tail.** Use the approved bold cover for approximately 0.3 seconds, rounded to whole output frames (nine frames at 30 fps). Tushar updated this on 2026-10-10 because the one-frame tail was too short to select in the cover slider. Keep the standalone JPG and verify the encoded duration and final frame. A reviewer recommendation cannot silently remove the cover or override a user preference. Polish covers under the thumbnail playbook while preserving Tushar's likeness.

## Truth and quality gates

Never invent a shell crack, audible crunch, child reaction, comparison win, or first-ever rating. Personal taste scores must come from Tushar. Do not turn “I changed whey and liked the texture” into “less whey caused the texture.” Distinguish a cookie-inspired flavor from an actual cookie ingredient.

Preserve original food and skin colors and sharpness. No full-frame wash, color layer, routine grade, or creative LUT. Inspect source HDR metadata and use the established deliberate color pipeline; a technical color conversion is not permission to stylize footage. Check source, working master, and encoded output.

After camera geometry or technical SDR/HDR conversion, verify transfer, primaries and matrix on every derived segment as well as the combined master. An encoder can inherit or drop source color tags despite requested output flags. Correct metadata to match the actual converted pixels; never retag unconverted SDR as HLG. Stream-copy concatenation must not carry conflicting segment color descriptions into the final picture.

Inspect actual pixels after every source boundary in a mixed-source master. Independently valid HEVC segments can still produce corrupt chroma when stream-copied together with incompatible parameter sets; metadata and successful decoding alone do not catch that. If the combined frame differs from the same frame decoded in its standalone segment, use the established independent-decoder concat path and verify again.

Inspect cut boundaries for stutter, duplicate/stale frames, and decoder stalls. Distinguish a still source shot from playback failure. Use the established continuous-master workflow when appropriate, and validate through the actual export path. Browser validation is not proof of final encoding quality or retention.

Measure loudness from the exact decoded audio used by the preview and final mix, not waveform height, fader position, or a reviewer's claimed LUFS. Use measured two-pass mastering when warranted and verify the resulting integrated loudness and true peak. A chosen −14 LUFS target is a production choice, not a guaranteed platform requirement or retention improvement. Preserve Marcus's expression; do not add tonal processing without evidence.

Trim silence against word alignment and the actual audio, not a screenshot interval alone. A proposed gap may contain the last word of a sentence. If speeding a preparation segment, retime picture, pitch-preserved voice, captions, and cards together. A faster static shot does not create a missing transformation. Use modest punch-ins only where the food action remains fully legible; camera emphasis is not proof of an unfilmed crack.

## How to accumulate learning

Append dated entries; do not overwrite the evidence behind older decisions. For each reviewed video, record:

- Video ID, version, source feedback, and the observed problem.
- Accepted/adapted/rejected suggestions and what changed.
- Evidence status: **inspection finding**, **creative hypothesis**, **user preference**, or **measured outcome**.
- Preview/export approval and any actual results, with measurement window and organic/promoted distinction.
- The reusable lesson, limits, and next test.

User approval means the edit was accepted, not that retention improved. When actual results arrive, add them to the existing entry. Promote a lesson to a standing rule only when Tushar explicitly makes it a rule or repeated evidence supports it; retain uncertainty and contrary results. Keep future shoots separate from changes achievable with existing footage.
