# Recipe-led long-form continuity — confirmed November 1, 2026

These rules capture the Halloween Creami review. Apply them to future packages; preserve approved releases.

- Teach the recipe as the action happens. Narrate measured ingredients, blending, freezing, first processing, mix-in addition, second processing and toppings in their real order. Personal stories fill natural process gaps; they must not displace the method or run over unrelated actions.
- Audit spoken phrases against source actions after every timing change. Do not describe brownie disintegration before the brownie has been added and processed. Distinguish first-spin texture from post-mix-in results. Explain measuring, melting and pouring at the corresponding shots. Never infer a different liquid or ingredient from an ambiguous container.
- When switching recipes or pints, Marcus closes the finished one and names the next: for example, “That's the finished [flavor] pint. Next up is [next flavor].” Approve the exact new words before paid recording and use the existing episode spending cap. No new cap is implied.
- Reinforce the switch visually: a brief advance notice, a numbered flavor reveal aligned with the spoken introduction, and a small unobtrusive flavor identity during the chapter. The approved example used a 3.5-second advance notice and 4.5-second reveal. Keep active food and hands visible; no silent full-screen title hold. A short purposeful finished-food callback may support the spoken closeout; document its source reuse rather than claiming exclusivity.
- Marcus always uses provider speed 1.0 and timeline speed 1.0. Keep internal and inter-topic narration pauses at or below one second. Do not slow speech or add silence to reach runtime. Measure the assembled audio after insertions; intentional branded intro and music-backed ending are separate scenes.
- Pronounce the plural Creamis as “creamies” / cream-eez. Keep Creami/Creamis spelling in public captions. Keep pronunciation substitutions private beside requests and alignment.
- Add newly supplied footage to the source manifest with actual file count, hashes and provenance. Rebuild action matching and captions; do not invent a missing clip. Mixed footage and a single 4K insert do not establish an all-native-4K film.
- Preserve the supplied branded intro, music, engagement assets and native end-screen contract. Verify intro and outro audio in the assembled program and encoded export, not only by the presence of audio tags or source files. If browser seeks miss independent audio elements, a continuous mixed program track is an acceptable preview/delivery implementation; retain original stems, timing, gain and fades. Signal-level checks prove signal/headroom, not a listening pass or device playback.
- Keep approved narration, pronunciation fixes, bridge takes, alignment, safe billing receipts and superseded editable revisions. Never pay for unchanged narration again merely to rebuild the picture.
- Validate nutrition against the exact filmed quantities and brand labels. PB2 and PBfit may be interchangeable in the creator's cooking but are not automatically nutrition-identical. Keep confirmed corrections with project evidence, and omit unverified totals or incomplete nutrient fields rather than showing zero.
- Review the main, three distinct promos, thumbnail likeness, covers, captions, Facebook copy and native Story-card artwork together. Save exact approval scope. Recording/preview approval does not authorize final exports; export approval does not authorize upload/scheduling. Carry explicit later authorization forward without asking again.

Current approved package details (actual recipe quantities, scores, family stories, credit costs and clip IDs) belong in private project records, not as universal recipe facts.
