## 1. Creator And Brand Context

The creator is Tushar Sharma. The brand is **The Split Plate**.

Core promise:

> Help busy parents make food, family life, and fitness work together through meals and shortcuts they will actually repeat.

Signature dinner method:

> **One cook, two plates.** Start from a shared cook, then adapt spice, sauce, serving style, or presentation for adults and kids.

Important personal context that can support truthful stories:

- Tushar works from home, trains, handles school pickups and family demands, and edits content as a second job, often beginning around 11 PM.
- For roughly six years, the family often cooked separately: kids ate around 5:30 PM, adults around 8 PM after bedtime, and dinner could consume three hours. The family intentionally moved toward eating together with one shared cook and two plate styles.
- Kids generally receive normal growing-years food, not automatically fat-free, keto, or macro-optimized substitutes. Their adaptations are commonly simpler seasoning, deconstruction, smaller portions, normal cheese/tortillas/bread, or sauce on the side.
- The audience should see a real household system, not a polished fantasy.

## 2. Target Audience And Geographic Packaging

Primary audience:

- Parents ages **25-45**.
- Primarily the United States, with Australia, the United Kingdom, Canada, and other developed English-speaking markets.
- They care about eating well and staying active but have limited time, work pressure, family logistics, and different preferences around the table.

Packaging implications:

- Put a truthful parent-life signal in the opening when it is part of the story: school pickup, dinner before an 11 PM edit shift, after-bedtime cleanup, lunchbox reality, workday pressure, or a home-gym routine.

## 3. Content Strategy

Every post has exactly one primary job:

| Job | Viewer value | Typical CTA | Primary measure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reach | Useful tip, surprise, or demonstration | Watch/share/explore | Non-follower reach, retention, shares |
| Growth | A recognizable series worth returning for | Follow/profile visit | Follows and profile conversion |
| Community | A real experience or worthwhile conversation | Reply/participate | Substantive comments and repeat participation |
| Monetization | Evidence that informs a purchase | View details/click/buy | Qualified clicks, conversions, revenue |

Six connected pillars:

1. Meals that fit real life.
2. Useful food knowledge.
3. Experiments and discovery.
4. Desserts and enjoyable routines.
5. The person behind the system.
6. Products that earn their place.

Do not relabel a recipe, ranking, carousel, or product appearance as Community merely because it asks a question. A product appearance is not Monetization unless the post answers a purchase question.

## 4. Approval Gates

These gates are mandatory:

1. Inspect all supplied footage before claiming what it contains.
2. Ask for missing servings, portions, ingredients, cook times, adult/kid split, sauce details, or macro inputs when accuracy depends on them.
3. Draft the narrative, timed Marcus script, clip plan, and on-screen text plan.
4. Show them to Tushar for approval before generating narration or rendering the narrated final.
5. Apply every correction to both the spoken narrative and the matching visual sequence.
6. Render only after approval.
7. Verify the final video technically and visually.
8. Package and inventory the exact approved version.

`LGTM` or `approve` applies to the immediately presented script/plan. It is not permission to ignore later corrections.

## 5. Video Editing Rules

### Opening

- Never open a social video with a static plate, website, card, tablet, or still-looking shot.
- Start with motion already happening: sauce pour, dip, bite, crunch, scoop, cheese pull, pan action, plate entering frame, or another sensory proof.
- The first three seconds should communicate value before a series name or episode number.
- When supported, combine: moving visual proof, short sound-off text, and complementary narration. Do not make all three layers repeat the same words.
- Calories and protein are a strong hook and should appear at the beginning of adult and Split Plate recipe videos when verified.

### Pacing

- Do not force every video under 30 seconds.
- Simple recipes commonly land around 30-60 seconds. Complex dinners can approach 60-90 seconds when the explanation needs room.
- Full-day `What I Eat` videos may run up to roughly two minutes when they genuinely show everything consumed.
- Remove dead air and duplicate actions, but do not make Marcus sound rushed or strip the story of emotion.
- Use readable 1.5-2.5 second action beats where appropriate.
- Place a meaningful visual change or micro-payoff approximately every 6-10 seconds in longer videos.
- Keep natural sound credible. Do not speed every ASMR action merely to shorten the runtime.
- Give differentiated sauces, dips, and toppings a real visual chapter rather than compressing them into an unexplained shot.
- Voiceover must match the action currently on screen. A correct recipe with mismatched visuals is not approved.

### Color, Framing, And Quality

- Social outputs are portrait `1080x1920` unless another format is explicitly requested.
- Correct red/orange front-camera casts before delivery. Check skin tone, food color, contrast, white balance, and brightness.
- Do not over-polish food until it looks fake.
- Reduce long full-body shots when they are unflattering or distract from the story; use tighter framing or shorter exposure to that angle.
- Use the strongest actual food action, not a weak generic shot, for teasers and openings.
- Verify first frame, contact sheet, duration, orientation, audio, and file playback.

### Text

- Keep overlays short, high contrast, and normally top-middle, clear of platform controls and food.
- One opening promise is enough: macros, time, texture, transformation, or family-system value.
- Do not bake dense ingredient paragraphs into video.
- When Tushar says text will be added in TikTok, deliver a clean visual edit and put the exact text plan in the package instead.
- Do not use opaque blocks that hide the food unless explicitly approved.

### Audio

- Preserve original cooking sound by default.
- Under Marcus, original sound is usually retained quietly rather than removed.
- Do not bake in music unless requested; Tushar commonly adds current audio in TikTok.

## 6. Marcus Voiceover Rules

- Marcus is the saved upbeat American social voice used when requested.
- Scripts need performance direction when emotion matters: `[warm]`, `[playful]`, `[curious]`, `[chuckles]`, or a short pause.
- Do not produce flat, emotionless Marcus for rankings, jokes, reactions, or personal stories.
- Do not rush Marcus merely to hit an arbitrary duration.
- Explain the purpose, tradeoff, family system, or ingredient logic. Do not read every measurement by default.
- Exact quantities can live in the caption/description and recipe page, especially for custom marinades and sauces.
- Brand names belong in narration when the brand materially affects the recipe, macro result, texture, identity, or recommendation. Example: Bootylicious is essential to the 30/30 Muffin Mug Bowl identity.
- Adult-focused dinner videos do not need to say `adult plate`; it is assumed when the kids' plate is not shown.
- Every Marcus video includes **exactly one natural CTA**.

CTA guidance:

- Like: after a relatable, funny, surprising, or sustained-value moment.
- Save: for a recipe, technique, or must-try result.
- Follow: after the viewer has received enough value and the continuing series is clear.
- Comment: for one genuine, answerable question.

A preferred mid-video pattern for longer videos is:

> By the way, if you've stayed this long, give it a like. It helps a lot.

Use it around the 30-second mark when natural, but rotate phrasing so videos do not sound templated. Do not stack `like + save + follow + comment` in one video.

## 7. Dinner Package: Four Required Videos

When enough distinct footage exists, a dinner package is incomplete until all four versions are built, verified, linked, and separately inventoried:

1. **Split Plate main:** the shared cook plus adult/kid decision and both outcomes.
2. **Adult-focused main:** the complete adult recipe, macro payoff, and adult visual story. Do not announce that it is the adult version.
3. **Under-15 discovery/comment-recipe cut:** a distinct fast visual spine, adult macros when relevant, and the purpose-specific recipe CTA.
4. **7-second teaser:** finished-dish B-roll and macros only.

Dinner timing rules:

- Teaser: normally one to three days before the Split Plate main, in the same week.
- Under-15 follow-up: roughly 7-14 days after the main.
- Adult-focused version: approximately 14 days after the actual main-post date.

Source separation:

- Allocate footage before editing.
- Raw source-time ownership is exclusive across Split Plate main, adult main, under-15 cut, teaser, and reserve clips.
- Do not create shorts by trimming the main timeline.
- Clean and text-overlay exports of the same timeline are the only expected overlap exception.
- Keep source-use manifests and run overlap checks.

Teaser rule, saved explicitly by Tushar:

> A teaser is B-roll on the final plated dish with calories and protein listed. It should be one strong, readable finished-product shot or a small set of final-plate B-roll, not process footage and not a miniature recipe.

## 8. Recurring Series Rules

### CrumblCreamiCut

- Weekly sequence is generally ranking, teaser, and three flavor videos.
- Use the actual weekly Crumbl flavor names and preserve the defining flavor elements.
- Saved base: 1 cup Fairlife 2% ultra-filtered milk, 2 tbsp monk fruit/erythritol sweetener, 1 serving unflavored whey isolate, 1/8 tsp guar gum, pinch of salt.
- Freeze upright and level for 24 hours, rest five minutes, then use Regular Ice Cream.
- Do not default to a hot-water bath.
- Machine mix-ins normally go in after the first spin and use Respin under the saved no-water method.
- Save face reactions where they add honest evaluation, especially for rankings, but a flavor video may also use one when enough distinct footage remains.
- Ranking videos begin with the ranking, give one score and reason per flavor, then one cross-flavor learning and one CTA.
- Rankings need a fresh visual spine and emotional Marcus delivery. They do not have to be under 30 seconds.

### Viral Recipes Proteinized

- Verify that a trend is actually current before building.
- Credit the originating creator when reasonably discoverable.
- Preserve the element that made the original viral.
- Explain what stayed, what changed, and why.
- Include toppings, sauce, garnish, and the served form in macros.
- End with an honest verdict, not automatic praise.
- Fudgy cheesecake bars correction: after freezing, refrigerate at least one hour for the firm-but-soft clean bite; do not present the frozen bite as the intended texture.

### Can I Make A Recipe Go Viral?

- This is for Tushar's original creation, not a copied trend.
- Frame it as a creation and report honestly; do not repeatedly call it a test when Tushar considers it a finished original.
- Current recent example: French Dip Polenta Dunkers with lemon-pepper beans. It was described as Tushar's best from-scratch creation so far.

### Quick Lunches

- Keep the problem and speed clear.
- Verify macro density when that is the promise.
- Product flexibility can be stated honestly, for example recommending a preferred fillet while acknowledging the filmed brand also works.
- Do not alter the linked recipe unless Tushar specifically asks for a recipe update.

### Product Reviews

- A genuine-use review must answer: why purchased, repeated use, use cases, strengths, drawbacks, who it fits, and whether it is sponsored/affiliate/gifted.
- Show package and nutrition panel when the claim depends on them.
- Do not imply sponsorship. Use `purchased by me`, `not sponsored`, and `no affiliate` where true.
- Multi-SKU use is valuable proof when reviewing a product family such as HighKey cookies.

### Community And Household Stories

- Use real details, not generic advice.
- Kitchen-reset story: dinner ends, kids go to bed, Tushar and his spouse take nearly two hours of peace/Netflix and ice cream before his 11 PM editing shift; cleanup happens the next morning. Tushar cooks and cleans the butcher block/stove, his wife usually handles the dishwasher, and the joke is declaring victory while the sink remains dirty. Use `spouse`, not generic `wife`, when broad audience wording is intended.
- Home-gym story: exercise became more realistic after investing in a garage gym rather than spending time traveling to a gym.
- Sustainable-lifestyle story: some dinners are frozen pizza and are not visually perfect or high protein; make reasonable choices and return to normal rather than treating one meal as failure.
- Community questions should be simple, answerable, and grounded in a visible real habit.

### What I Eat

- Show everything consumed in chronological order when building a full-day video.
- Use actual times, actual portions, and the final calorie/protein total after the day is complete.
- Recovery/rest days should be described honestly.
- Do not mention a meal while unrelated coffee footage is on screen.
- If recipe footage is incomplete, say the recipe video is dropping soon instead of inserting an editing note into the video.
- Humor is welcome when truthful; use `spouse` for broadly relatable copy.

## 9. Reelrise Lessons To Apply Selectively

Treat Reelrise suggestions as hypotheses and editing prompts, not unquestionable facts.

Useful retained patterns:

- **ASMR macro-pour/texture hook:** tight pour, crack, scoop, sizzle, or texture proof; macro overlay at the payoff; no forced music.
- **Split-Plate recipe remix:** familiar meal, visible adult/kid adaptation, clear reason for the split.
- **Three-layer sound-off hook:** moving visual, concise high-contrast text, and complementary narration.
- **Dinner rescue open loop:** establish the real problem, show the practical fix, then deliver the meal payoff. Do not manufacture crises or withhold the useful result only to force comments.
- **Lunchbox reality check:** show normal, practical food without teacher conflict, parent shaming, or diet-culture bait.
- **Stacked micro-payoffs:** give viewers a useful visual change every several seconds and remove dead air.

Avoid fear hooks such as `Stop feeding your kids this`. Do not shame regular food, children, spouses, teachers, or parents for engagement.

## 10. Captions, Hashtags, And Calls To Action

- Start captions with the strongest plain-English value or result.
- Use a compact recipe block only when useful.
- Keep the audience signal near the beginning when the post is parent-led.
- Use one low-friction question or action.
- Prefer 4-5 specific searchable hashtags in current website/social packages; the saved general rules may contain older 5-8 guidance, so follow the latest platform/package convention for the task.
- Avoid generic hashtag walls.
- Do not use a bare option list as the spoken ending. Ask a complete question.
- When pointing to the website, follow the approved wording for that package. Current saved rules favor `Exact measurements are in the description. Visit thesplitplate.com.` with the site visible at the end; older packages may say `link in bio`. Do not silently mix conventions.

## 11. Carousels

- Carousels are separate publishable assets, even if the recipe video was already posted.
- A new carousel can follow an older posted recipe as a searchable/saveable repurpose.
- Standard target is seven concise cards when the recipe supports it: hero, ingredients, method, serving/payoff, and save/visit end card.
- Use clean hero macros, intentional photos, concise card text, and recipe-specific captions.
- Never solve overflow by hiding text under another element. Shorten copy or paginate.
- Do not use package/brand images as social hero cards unless the package itself is the story.
- Generate and inspect a contact sheet. Check every card at its real square dimensions.
- Do not schedule two carousels on the same day.
- Inventory a carousel only after the live route is deployed and verified.
- Existing recipe posted + newer recipe/video version: keep both records. Mark the old version posted, create a distinct new-version asset with its own date, angle, canonical file, and status, and schedule only when the new visual spine or recipe experience is materially different.

## 12. YouTube Rules

- Create a `1920x1080` landscape project for portrait source footage; aspect-ratio changes cannot recover scenery outside the original recording.
- Fit portrait footage to frame height by default.
- Use the extra space for useful recipe notes, ingredient logic, warnings, verdicts, or a second relevant clip.
- Other approved layouts: blurred duplicate background, two clips side by side, or selective full-screen crop when the action survives the crop.
- Use landscape close-ups and horizontal introductions in future shoots when available.
- A long-form recipe can add method explanation, what can go wrong, substitutions, and relevant footage from other verified-unused sources.
- YouTube is a distinct edit and may reuse owned recipe footage across platforms, but still needs coherent visual planning and internal source tracking.
- Package and inventory the YouTube export separately.

## 15. Recipe And Macro Honesty

- User corrections override older recipe-page data and earlier generated scripts.
- Verify claims that affect health, nutrition, product comparison, or safety.
- Use package labels and official sources when claims depend on a branded product.
- Calculate the complete served dish, including cooking fats, sauces, cheese, toppings, bread/tortillas, and mix-ins.
- State estimates honestly. Keep calories conservative only when the underlying inputs support the lower estimate; do not omit meaningful ingredients to make macros look better.
- Adult macros belong to the adult serving. Do not prescribe children's calories or macros without an explicit request.
- If a user asks the agent to `check` a value, do not mention the checking process in the video. Report only the verified result or keep it in the handoff notes.
- For custom sauces/marinades, list exact ingredients in the caption/description even when narration stays concise.
- Respect dietary questions carefully. For `Is this halal?`, distinguish the recipe concept from specific ingredient certification and advise checking gelatin, enzymes, meat sourcing, and package certification rather than making an unsupported blanket claim.

## 17. Storage And Cleanup

- Canonical project outputs and manifests are more important than duplicate Downloads copies.
- Zips are delivery snapshots. Once the website is live, the canonical source/package exists, and the zip is older than 30 days, older redundant zips may be removed.
- Alternate exports older than 30 days may be removed when clearly superseded, rejected, and not referenced by inventory.
- Do not delete approved canonical MP4s, clean masters still needed for platform reuse, source manifests, recipe handoffs, reserve clips, or inventory evidence.
- Do not delete raw footage unless explicitly authorized and verified against all current/future variants.
- Preserve non-overlapping reserve footage even when storage is tight.
- Before cleanup, report candidate paths and expected recovered space, then delete only the approved set.

## 20. Final Operating Principle
