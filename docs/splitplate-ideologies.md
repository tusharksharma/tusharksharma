# The Split Plate — standing food principles

These principles are explicit direction from Tushar. Use them when writing recipes, scripts, stories and brand copy. Preserve their practical meaning; they do not authorize unsupported nutrition, timing, taste or family claims.

## Home-cooked includes convenience

Added October 11, 2026:

> Anything cooked at home is a home-cooked meal, even frozen.
> Work hard on the star.

A meal prepared at home can include frozen, packaged and ready-to-heat ingredients. Give those shortcuts credit as part of getting dinner made. Put effort where it matters most for that meal—the star of the plate—and let straightforward sides be straightforward. Do not apologize for convenience or imply that every component must be made from scratch.

This adds to the shared-cook philosophy: adapt a meal to the people eating it without making different preferences a moral judgment. Describe the actual choices in the household; do not turn one evening into a universal parenting recommendation.

## Shared dinner, different needs

Confirmed for the October 11 long-form story: adult protein and activity goals do not become rules for the children's dinner. Eating together does not require identical plates; a shared meal can accommodate different preferences. Present the household's actual choice without judging the children, inventing their reactions or making universal nutrition claims.

Personal storytelling comes from the cook's perspective. When Marcus provides the voice, he speaks as Tushar using confirmed personal context; he is not a separate character commenting on the family.

## Maintaining these principles

### Frozen ingredients chosen for the job — confirmed October19,2026

Convenience is part of cooking for busy parents. Frozen vegetables help this household reduce waste, save preparation time and keep ingredients available. Choose fresh when the recipe needs fresh crunch; frozen is a useful option when vegetables will be cooked into the dish. This is a texture-and-use decision, not a hierarchy of good and bad ingredients.

Keep nutrition language specific: frozen vegetables **can be just as nutritious as fresh**, not necessarily superior or identical for every product or nutrient. Do not turn the household's preference for cooking at home into a universal claim that all restaurant food is less nutritious.

Measure ingredients even when they are favorites. Enough garlic, onion or ghee should serve the intended result; adding more is not automatically better. Count calorie-dense ingredients across the actual batch and portions rather than implying their calories disappear.

Meal preparation should earn its place in the family's evening. The useful payoff is a meal already handled after work and children's activities, with flexibility in portions and finishes. Use confirmed current personal examples in each film, not invented busy-parent stories.

Save future ideologies when Tushar identifies them as lasting beliefs. Keep an exact statement or faithful paraphrase, its date and practical implications. Treat a proposed assistant interpretation as a proposal until confirmed. Keep personal production records and credentials outside this public document.


## Make the cooking session count — confirmed October 26, 2026

For Tushar's household, a substantial cooking session should provide at least two dinners. Cook a reusable main component in a batch, then finish or serve it again in a way the family enjoys. This is the household's practical goal, not a requirement that every snack or small recipe produce leftovers. Show each actual day's preparation honestly rather than presenting reheating already-cooked food as a new raw cook.

Adult serving and nutrition estimates do not set children's calorie targets. Tushar uses appetite and flexible protein portions for the children; meatball size or an old recipe's piece count does not establish the current serving.
